# Visible-Light-Mediated Continuous-Flow C–H Functionalization of Arenes in a Coil Photoreactor

作者：Wei Zhang, Lin Chen, Hao Liu, Mei Wang

通讯作者：Mei Wang (mei.wang@example.edu)

## Abstract

We report a continuous-flow protocol for the visible-light-mediated C–H functionalization of electron-rich arenes using a coiled photoreactor irradiated by blue LEDs. Under optimized conditions the model reaction proceeded to 91% isolated yield with a residence time of 12 min, and the observed rate constant for the title transformation was k_obs = 2.4 × 10^-3 s^-1 at 25 °C. A series of 14 substrates was examined, and the method tolerated esters, nitriles, and protected amines. The protocol was readily scaled to 25 mmol without loss of efficiency. These results establish a practical platform for photochemical C–H functionalization in flow.

## 1. Introduction

Direct C–H functionalization has emerged as a powerful strategy for constructing complex molecules from simple precursors [@doi:10.1038/nature14539]. Although numerous thermal protocols have been developed, many suffer from poor selectivity and require stoichiometric oxidants. Photochemical activation offers an attractive alternative because the reactive intermediates can be generated under mild conditions. However, batch photochemistry is often limited by poor light penetration and uneven irradiation. Continuous-flow reactors address these limitations by reducing the optical path length and enabling precise control over the residence time.

Herein we describe a coiled continuous-flow photoreactor for the C–H functionalization of arenes. The key rate law governing the transformation is shown in Equation (1):

$$\frac{d[\mathrm{C}]}{dt} = -k_{\mathrm{obs}}[\mathrm{A}][\mathrm{B}]$$

The temperature dependence of the observed rate constant follows the Arrhenius relationship:

$$k_{\mathrm{obs}} = A\exp\!\left(-\frac{E_a}{RT}\right)$$

where E_a is the apparent activation energy and R is the gas constant. This kinetic framework guided our optimization of the flow parameters.

## 2. Experimental Section

### 2.1 Reactor Setup

The reactor consisted of a perfluoroalkoxy (PFA) coil (1.0 mm i.d., 5.0 m length) wrapped around a water-cooled aluminum cylinder. Irradiation was provided by six blue LEDs (450 nm) mounted on the inner surface of the housing. Reagents were delivered by two syringe pumps and mixed in a T-junction immediately before entering the coil. The schematic of the setup is shown in Figure 1.

![Figure 1. Schematic of the continuous-flow coiled photoreactor used in this study.](projects/mock-paper/images/fig-reactor-setup.jpg)

### 2.2 General Procedure

A solution of the arene (1.0 equiv) and the coupling partner (1.5 equiv) in acetonitrile was pumped through the reactor at a total flow rate of 0.42 mL/min. The outlet stream was collected, concentrated, and purified by flash chromatography. The observed rate constant was determined by in-line monitoring of the conversion over time.

## 3. Results and Discussion

### 3.1 Optimization of Reaction Conditions

We first examined the effect of residence time and light intensity on the model reaction. Increasing the residence time from 4 to 12 min improved the yield from 58% to 91%, whereas longer residence times led to a slight decrease due to product decomposition. The optimized conditions are summarized in Table 1.

**Table 1.** Optimization of the continuous-flow C–H functionalization of anisole.

| Entry | Residence time (min) | Light intensity (%) | Yield (%) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 4 | 50 | 58 |
| 2 | 8 | 50 | 77 |
| 3 | 12 | 50 | 91 |
| 4 | 12 | 100 | 88 |
| 5 | 16 | 50 | 84 |

The reaction exhibited clean first-order kinetics in the arene, as shown by the linear time course in Figure 2. Fitting the data gave k_obs = 2.4 × 10^-3 s^-1, consistent with the rate law given above.

![Figure 2. Kinetic time course for the model C–H functionalization reaction.](projects/mock-paper/images/fig-kinetics.jpg)

### 3.2 Substrate Scope

With the optimized conditions in hand, we explored the scope of the transformation. Electron-rich arenes bearing methyl, methoxy, and halo substituents were converted in good to excellent yields. The results are collected in Table 2.

**Table 2.** Substrate scope of the visible-light-mediated C–H functionalization.

| Entry | Substrate | Product | Yield (%) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Anisole | 4-substituted anisole | 91 |
| 2 | 1,3-Dimethoxybenzene | 4-substituted arene | 87 |
| 3 | N,N-Dimethylaniline | para-functionalized aniline | 82 |
| 4 | Toluene | benzyl-functionalized arene | 64 |

The method is complementary to thermal C–H functionalization and expands the accessible chemical space [@doi:10.1103/PhysRevLett.116.061102]. Electron-poor arenes such as nitrobenzene were unreactive under these conditions, which is consistent with the proposed single-electron-transfer mechanism.

## 4. Conclusion

We have developed a continuous-flow protocol for the visible-light-mediated C–H functionalization of arenes. The coiled photoreactor enabled rapid optimization and provided high yields with short residence times. The kinetic analysis supports a first-order dependence on the arene, and the method tolerated a range of functional groups. Extension of this platform to other photochemical transformations is currently underway in our laboratory.
